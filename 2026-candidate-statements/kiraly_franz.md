## Candidate statement - Franz Kiraly

GC.OS has grown since its foundation in 2024 into a significant independent open source AI organization.

I have successfully steered and grown GC.OS through its founding phase, and am now proud to see the organization maturing its representative structure.

I'd be happy for the opportunity to also help the organization move through its next, exciting phase.

In a world where AI and open source increasingly get monopolized, GC.OS is more important than ever in providing everyone with free access to a common foundation layer for AI.


### Political vision - open source as a democractic governance model

Open source is a major economic factor worldwide - especially for the AI revolution.

Therefore, thinking about governance - and ensuring that it is democratic and benefitting all - is crucial. See this [interview with me on the topic](https://www.odbms.org/2025/11/on-the-economic-and-political-aspects-of-open-source-qa-with-franz-kiraly/).

GC.OS needs to become one of a network of organizations doing the important work of building sustainable governance and innovation models for AI.

The focus needs to be on models that actually work on scale - close to societal and political systems in the EU and unaligned middle powers.

#### The tactical situation

While the ultimate goal should be an integration with societal or governmental processes, we had to learn in the last years that - despite a lot of lip service - the actual politics currently often does not favour our approach.

For example, in Germany, the problem is not lack of funding for open source, the yearly budget is in the likely range of billions. However, misallocatio is rampant, and so are incompetence, corruption, and lobbying.

Technology funding in the AI space is frequently allocated by individuals, companies and (registered) lobby organizations without a democratic mandate. This is often tolerated - or even directly initiated - by the politicians on point (in Germany: mostly CDU/CSU).

Therefore, we - the open source communities - must become sustainable and scale on our own. The fight for a democratic future takes place exactly here. We should of course be able to quickly make any external support count, but we must not rely on it, or expect it.

This can be achieved, for instance, by widening our professional fundraising activities, and building commercial revenue sources.

Simultaneously, we need to give a voice to the scattered open source communities and users of AI, integrate them, and become an "anti-lobby", promoting good governance, anti-corruption, and an allocation of governmental technology funding that actually benefits the democratic sovereign and taxpayer - the people(s) of Germany, the EU, and the unaligned powers.

This also means taking the side of everyday users of AI, as well as public and private sector organizations who primarily use AI to further their core business.

In particular, this also implies that investments and cooperations in the commercial playing field must be strategic, following business models, services, and products that empower societies and users, rather than disempowering them, for instance, via lock-in, or closed technologies.


### Contributions and achievements

Over the last years, I have been instrumental in:

* setting up the operational and legal framework for GC.OS
* making GC.OS the well-known AI open source umbrella organizations that it is today
* designing the current and future governance framework, including the representative council
* fundraising and sustainability, e.g., establishing European Summer of Code, joint venture companies


### Policies proposed for 2026-2028

Proposed goals are based on the tactical situation assessment and proposed long-term strategy:

* reach a continuous level of financial sustainability that allows to support a small operation - with 10-50 people in the wider network
* expand scope and volume of opportunity generation for talented juniors, expand volume of regular stipend programmes (e.g., European Summer of Code)
* widen the network and help build similar organizations in other jurisdictions, where possible - e.g., India, the Netherlands, or Spain
* become a recognized voice in national and supranational politics that shows a different model for AI governance is possible
* ramp up activities and also expand our function as a public interest watchdog around funding misallocation and wasteage in the open source space
